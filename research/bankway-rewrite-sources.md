# Bankway section enrichment

## Sources used

- **Buffalo Industrial Bank advertisement, Dziennik Dla Wszystkich, 27 October 1945, p. 5.** Read the original page and the existing repository transcription/translation in `bankway_research/buffalo-industrial-bank-ad-translations.md`. The advertisement illustrates a 1945 Credit and Identification Card, promotes credit across independent dealers in western New York, offers the card without a fee, provides a dealer booklet, and invites existing borrowers to request a card by mail. It claims 25 years of earlier merchandise financing and encourages retaining war bonds while using credit.
- **Associated Press dispatch, Buffalo, 14 November 1945, Oklahoma City Times, p. 1.** [Oklahoma Historical Society scan and OCR](https://gateway.okhistory.org/ark:/67531/metadc1760931/m1/1/). Reports a bank announcement on that date and explains annual renewal, prior credit establishment, telephone verification, assignment of instalment contracts, and collection by the bank. October's local advertisement predates this national report; neither establishes the exact first issuance date.
- **“Bank Inaugurates New Credit Plan For Installment Buyers,” The Berrien County Record, 6 December 1945, p. 3.** [Buchanan District Library issue](https://www.buchananlibrary.org/files/542d501d1/1945-12-06_BCR.pdf). Read the digitised issue's extracted text. Confirms approximately 200 dealers, merchandise categories, operating steps and vice president Kenneth R. Reid's precedence claim. The web screenshot request failed; the article's location and contents are available in the PDF text.
- **Sgt. George Toles, “New Credit Plan,” Radio News, October 1946, p. 141.** [Digitised issue](https://www.worldradiohistory.com/Archive-Radio-News/40s/Radio-News-1946-10-R.pdf); local copy `bank_credit_cards/Radio-News-1946-10-R.pdf`, supplied by the user during this rewrite. Extracted the text and visually read PDF page 141, which is also printed p. 141. The article explicitly states payments were made separately on each instalment contract, and forecasts 600 dealers as consumer goods became available. The printed name is Kenneth R. Reid; OCR incorrectly reads Reed. OCR also reads the footer as 111, but the image clearly establishes 141. Direct archive downloads returned HTTP 403; the local copy resolved verification.
- **Later local advertisements:** `bankway_research/1951_04_17_005.pdf`, `1951_05_15_002.pdf`, and `1952_04_26_005.pdf`, with repository translations. Visually checked the April 1952 page. The Bankway slogan and Approved Dealer emblem survive; the ads principally concern other services and do not prove ongoing issuance of the 1945 card.

## Interpretation and limits

- Changed the exact 27 October launch claim to **advertised by that date**.
- Replaced the absolute first-bank-card claim with documented precedence over the 1946 Bay Shore Charg-It trial. Reid's first-of-its-kind claim remains attributed to him.
- Added free issuance, prior customers' mail requests, war-bond marketing, annual renewal, telephone verification, and the mechanics of contract financing.
- Clarified that each purchase had a separate instalment contract and payment obligation, rather than one consolidated monthly card balance. The radio-and-furniture example is an explanatory illustration, not a recorded transaction.
- Distinguished the approximately 200 dealers reported in December 1945 from the forecast of 600.
- Removed the unsubstantiated conclusion that the programme “worked.” Continued use of the Bankway brand through April 1952 is narrower evidence than success or unchanged continuation of the card scheme.
- Corrected the later Diners Club sentence calling Bankway a universal **charge card**, to keep the article consistent with the separate-contract model.
- Existing New York Times and Nation's Business references were retained. Nation's Business was not independently retrieved in this pass; the new text does not depend on that citation.
