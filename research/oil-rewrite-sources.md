# Oil-company card rewrite: sources and decisions

## Scope

Rewrote the Oil Companies section using the three new PDFs, the existing newspaper clipping, relevant passages from Mandell and Evans/Schmalensee, and targeted online verification. The section covers oil-company cards and their operating arrangements, through the 1952 Chevromatic improvement. It does not expand into the later bank-card schemes.

## New primary sources

1. **National Petroleum News, 25 April 1923, printed p. 61 (PDF p. 45).** “Oil Companies Issue Credit Cards / To Deserving Customers As Favors,” datelined Freeport, 20 April. Read extracted text and inspected the original page. Identifies Leader Petroleum of Freeport, Sweney Gasoline & Oil of Peoria, and Leader Oil of Dubuque. Establishes existing card issuance before 1924; describes wallet-sized credentials, approximately thirty days' credit, three-copy purchase tickets, monthly statements, credit references, and acceptance at stations and garages selling the issuer's brand. The issue's printed page and PDF sequence differ. [Archive issue](https://archive.org/details/sim_npn-national-petroleum-news_1923-04-25_15_17).
2. **National Petroleum News, 1 August 1923, printed p. 96 (PDF p. 60).** “Coupon Books Or Credit Cards? / Both Are Successful,” datelined Cleveland, 27 July. Read extracted text and inspected the original page. Louis Kuehnle says Leader Oil switched to credit cards “last November,” supporting November 1922. His statement describes signature identification, credit-rating checks, acceptance at company stations and dealers, cash reimbursement to dealers and collection from customers. Hawkeye's A. H. Caward describes $15/$30 prepaid coupon books, truck/driver restrictions and expense records. [Original page](https://archive.org/details/sim_npn-national-petroleum-news_1923-08-01_15_31/page/n59/mode/2up).
3. **Business Week, 3 May 1941, pp. 34, 36–37**, “More Credit Cards.” All three article pages visually checked. Source describes the sales-versus-credit-department debate, the cost of opening active accounts, card influence on cash sales, reciprocal coverage, Indiana Standard's newly completed U.S./Canadian coverage, Socony's approximately 80,000-station network with gaps, and proposed Independent Credit Cards arrangements. Kept projected operations explicitly prospective. Omitted the roughly 5% deduction, which the article labels an estimate rather than an announced fee. Did not extrapolate one company's application conversion figures to the whole industry.

## Existing sources

- **Lewis Mandell, The Credit Card Industry: A History (1990), pp. 18–23**, repository PDF: early oil-card rationale, monthly billing, dealer financing, wartime interruption, postwar revival and 1952 Chevromatic. The trial's 94% reduction in identification errors and halving of slip-completion time are retrospective reported figures, not a newly retrieved trial report.
- **Evans and Schmalensee, Paying with Plastic, 2nd ed., pp. 53–54**, repository PDF: broader context of prewar merchant cards and 1920s courtesy cards. Does not resolve a precise first oil-card issuer.
- **Existing 1928 Springfield Daily Republican clipping**: visually read. It describes discount pricing, not an explicit credit arrangement. Retained it with a new caption and an explanation of that distinction. Date/page remain the repository's attribution; the surrounding issue was not re-retrieved.
- **research/oil-credit-card.md**: used its source leads; its Texaco Story link did not yield a readable book in this pass, and the named download was not located in the repository.

## Online checks

- [Melso v. Texaco, 532 F. Supp. 1280 (1982)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp/532/1280/2145798/): findings say Texaco's program existed in various forms from 1914. Retained 1914 with explicit retrospective attribution; no original 1914 announcement found, nor a demonstrated original paper-card design.
- [American Heritage, “Credit Card America” (1991)](https://www.americanheritage.com/credit-card-america): retrospective General Petroleum 1924 date. Replaced the old claim that it issued the first gasoline credit card with the new 1922–23 contemporary evidence for other companies.
- [Collectors' history of Chevron cards](https://www.creditcollectibles.com/history-chevron-credit-card/): dates a reciprocal arrangement to July 1938 and asserts the first national card system. Neither the exact date nor precedence was independently established by the supplied primary sources. The rewrite instead describes reciprocal arrangements as documented by May 1941, with an explicit contemporary report of U.S./Canadian coverage.

## Corrections and limits

- Removed mutually inconsistent first-issuer claims and the unsupported Bellamy naming lineage.
- Distinguished regional suppliers from a single supposed 1924 origin.
- Qualified 1914 as retrospective evidence, and derived November 1922 narrowly from Kuehnle's statement.
- Described monthly charge accounts without implying revolving balances in the 1923 plans.
- Explained prepayment, deferred payment and discount cards separately.
- Avoided treating all programs as proven loss-makers; the 1941 source shows costs and conflicting commercial judgments.
- Used existing imagery; added no reconstructed historical card or portrait.
