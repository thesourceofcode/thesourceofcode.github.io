## Sources read

- C. Kenneth Fuller, “A Bank’s ‘Charg-It’ Plan for Merchants,” *Burroughs Clearing House*, November 1950, pp. 28–29, 74–75. All four newly supplied JPG pages in `bank_credit_cards/charg-it/` read visually. Primary account by Paterson’s president; central source for enrollment, costs, paperwork, terms, licensing and operating-market estimates.
- “Bank Starts First Credit Plan,” *Newsday*, 16 August 1946, p. 3. Existing `charg_it_launch_1946.jpg` read visually. Confirms Bay Shore inaugural introduction, preceding merchant meeting, Manufacturers Trust affiliation, original dual-bank proposal abandoned in favor of local operation, credit card/vouchers, approximate 20% income allowance and monthly repayment without customer interest/extra charges.
- Bay Shore bank advertisement, *Newsday*, 22 October 1946, p. 6. Existing `charg_it_ad_1946.jpg` read visually. Eleven stores, one itemized bill and combined payment.
- Sean H. Vanatta, *Plastic Capitalism* (2024), pp. 38–40 and chapter 2 notes 8–10, p. 311. Read existing extracted text. Corroborates Bay Shore first trial and Flatbush’s acquisition; possible conflict with Manufacturers Trust’s department-store clients is his conjecture. Also checked the supplied accepted manuscript of “Charge Account Banking,” pp. 10–12.
- Lewis Mandell, *The Credit Card Industry* (1990), p. 26. Read existing extracted passage; older Brooklyn/1947 chronology disagrees with the 1946 primary newspaper. Text explicitly distinguishes the accounts.
- “Charge It—With the Bank,” *Business Week*, 23 September 1950, pp. 58, 60. Existing transcription in `research/universal-card-research.md` consulted. Thirty merchants, scrip purchase procedure and odd-cent handling, 8% discount versus 4–6% department-store cost estimate, uncertainty over costs/break-even and 2,000–3,000-account target; Biggins joined Paterson in 1947.
- “Community Charge Account,” *Banking*, October 1950, p. 84. Existing transcription consulted for September 6 launch, late-August dinner, 25 stores and repayment descriptions. Its broad no-extra-cost language is qualified by Fuller’s more precise regular versus revolving terms.
- “New ‘Charg-It’ Plan in Use in New Jersey Simplifies Shopping for Store and Patron,” *New York Times*, 19 August 1951. Supplied single-page PDF rendered and read visually. Newark/Essex franchise, announced previous day, two repayment options. No page number supplied, so none invented.
- “‘Charg-It’ Plan Goes West,” *Banking*, August 1952, p. 71. Existing transcription consulted for fourth bank in then-current rollout, Bellevue, Newark and Jersey City. Its first-bank wording is not taken to negate the documented 1946 Bay Shore trial.

## Corrections and qualifications

1. Replaced the unsupported Flatbush trial/cancellation narrative with documented Bay Shore introduction. Retained Flatbush as the origin of Biggins’s work. No speculative claim that Manufacturers Trust executives were baffled; no father-sale anecdote carried forward.
2. Distinguished announcement/introduction from day of customer operation in 1946; no exact first-purchase date invented.
3. Removed absolute first-universal/revolving-card claims and the unqualified success conclusion. Bankway precedes this section, and Diners Club later achieved much wider acceptance.
4. Bay Shore’s reported 1946 interest-free monthly repayment is separate from Paterson’s two 1950 options. Fuller gives the regular account’s 25-day payment window and possible 0.5% monthly carried-balance charge on revolving accounts. That conditional charge is not presented as universal or automatically applied to every customer.
5. The $180/$30 example measures repayment against the approved line, not the outstanding balance. No claim made about modern minimum-payment formulas or how much newly available credit a particular payment restored.
6. Fuller gives immediate NET merchant settlement via transmittal forms; corrected former full-amount sentence. The 8% rate was introductory; 2% rebates depended on enrollment quotas. Post-January continuation was forecast, not a verified result.
7. Enrollment figures belong to different snapshots: 25 launch stores, 30 in September, 46 mid-October.
8. Fuller’s population/trading-area and $50,000–$1 million store-sales ranges are estimates, not eligibility rules. His aggressive-collections language is attributed.
9. Used the forms illustration already in the blog. Removed the staff-opening photo and the launch gallery from this section in keeping with the user’s preference against people photographs; existing image files/front-matter gallery definitions remain available elsewhere.
10. No web search needed: the supplied primary scans resolve the substantive chronology and operating questions. Preserved all text outside Charg-It and its seven new footnote definitions, including earlier Franklin and Amex edits.
