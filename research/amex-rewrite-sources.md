# American Express section: research and editorial decisions

Rewritten 1 October 2026 in `_posts/2026-05-15-payments-101-credit-card-chronology.md`.

## Sources read

- Matty Simmons, *The Credit Card Catastrophe* (1995), pp. 59–75 and 95: travelers cheque float; Clark, Townsend and Reed; proposed Diners Club acquisition; the launch campaign; application-display retaliation; credit losses; Waters and computer billing. This participant memoir supplies anecdotes, explicitly attributed in the article.
- Lewis Mandell, *The Credit Card Industry: A History* (1990), pp. 22–29: hotel and airline resistance, Universal Travelcard, Amex's defensive motive, first-year totals, international relationships and financial resources.
- Peter Z. Grossman, *American Express: The Unofficial History of the People Who Built the Great Financial Empire* (1987), pp. 267–288 and 299–303: acquisition negotiations, authorization and launch planning, inherited card lists, accounting problems, negative float, Waters, aborted 1961 merger and recovery. Printed page numbers differ from PDF indices by 30 in these passages.
- Daniel Gross et al., “American Express and the Charge Card,” *Forbes Greatest Business Stories of All Time* (1996), supplied excerpt PDF pp. 6–7: Colonial Stores and the 1963 overseas check-cashing benefit. This author is not Peter Z. Grossman.
- “Credit-Card Game,” *Time*, 22 September 1958, pp. 82, 84, supplied EBSCO export: contemporary prelaunch membership lists, bank distribution, international offices and quoted fees.
- Hillel Black, *Buy Now, Pay Later* (1961), pp. 27–28: manual account controls, telephone authorization and invalid-card lists.
- Evans and Schmalensee, *Paying with Plastic*, 2nd ed. (2004), pp. 57–59: background, float, merchant pricing and recovery. Do not copy its recovery-era fee figures without checking: the $39/$48 figures are expressed on a different price basis from the original $8/$10 figures in Grossman.
- Stearns, *Electronic Value Exchange* (2011), pp. 16–17: embossing and imprinting, travel offices and launch competition. Its compressed account omits the initial paper phase.

## Reconciled details

1. The 1956 proposed deal differs in structure between Simmons and Grossman. The narrative attributes Simmons's roughly $5 million founders' interests, rather than treating it as an agreed purchase price for the whole public company.
2. Grossman's inherited lists (approximately 150,000 hotel and more than 40,000 Gourmet members) differ from Time's 160,000 and 45,000. Both are identified; inherited lists are not treated as new paying membership or necessarily unique customers.
3. Time's six million forms before launch and Simmons's eight million invitations mailed in early October describe separate timings. Attribution is retained.
4. Time reports $6 initial membership / $4 renewal. The actual 30 September Cincinnati launch ad explicitly says $6 a year. The text reports the discrepancy rather than silently selecting one.
5. Merchant discounts varied. Simmons's typical 6 percent is not a uniform rate for every establishment; Evans and Schmalensee give restaurant and hotel ranges.
6. May 1959 is the plastic introduction. Simmons links plastic to Waters's later reforms, and some histories imply plastic from launch; original paper designs expiring April 1959 and the company history resolve that chronology. The Smithsonian's first-major-issuer claim is qualified against BankAmericard's September 1958 plastic launch.
7. The green redesign was in 1969, supported by Amex's own archivist. It should not be conflated with the 1959 plastic change.
8. Thirty days **overdue** is the collection threshold under Waters, not a thirty-day purchase-to-payment cycle.
9. The unsourced original 1965 “Cancellation Bulletin” and “Be My Guest” notes were replaced by Black's documented contemporary account of operational safeguards.

## Follow-up: the 1946 proposal

Added a subsection on the July 1946 business-travel-card suggestion, Eichelberger's late-1946 corporate plan, the $400–$500 security deposit, projected 5,000 firms / $20 million revenue / $1 million profit, Reed's initial support and eventual shelving. Source: Grossman, printed pp. 264–265 (PDF pp. 294–295), cross-checked against the supplied Forbes excerpt, PDF p. 4. Grossman identifies the deposit as security against default or abuse; Forbes compresses it into deposit-and-draw language. The article preserves that distinction rather than labeling the detailed proposal a modern debit card. The forecasts were not operating results, and no card was issued under this plan.

## Follow-up: announcement versus operation in 1958

Added the public announcement **by June 1958**, distinct from the **1 October** operational launch. Primary evidence is the existing 30 September 1958 Cincinnati Enquirer launch advertisement, p. 7A: “Since it was announced three months ago,” with the service beginning the following day. This supports approximately June, not a specific day. Grossman p. 284 records May press rumors and company confirmation, then application advertisements a few weeks later; the text preserves these stages and uses “by June” rather than claiming a single first announcement in June. Internet checks found contemporary summer reporting and the Gourmet acquisition, but no independently verified exact June announcement date.
