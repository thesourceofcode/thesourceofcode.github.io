# Interview with Mr. Jehangir Patel: Diners Club India, 1977–1991

## Purpose and approach

Understand how Diners Club India worked and changed during the Aggarwal years, drawing on Mr. Patel’s experience editing *Signature* from 1977 to 1991. Use the magazine as an entry into the business and its social world. His editorial role does not necessarily mean he had access to financial or operational decisions.

**Suggested opening:** “I’m researching the history of credit cards in India, particularly Diners Club. Your years editing Signature overlap with a major period of change. I’d like to hear what you saw, who was involved, and where the surviving records might be.”

Before starting, ask whether he is comfortable with recording and being quoted in the blog. During the conversation, distinguish what he witnessed from what others told him. Start with his recollections; introduce the dated evidence afterwards so it does not steer his answers.

## Follow-ups: members, merchants, and daily operations

15. **Can you recall a member’s experience that shows what the card made possible—or where it failed?** For example, a disputed restaurant bill, a refused card, an unexpected charge, or help while travelling. Can the account be used without identifying a private customer?


18. **How did payment processing change during your years there?** What do you remember of sales vouchers, card imprinters, telephone approvals, statements, and computers?
    - The draft associates computerisation with the post-1976 expansion, but the 1984 anniversary advertisement dates entering the “computer age” to 1983. Were there several stages, or is one account mistaken?
    - Who introduced the systems, and who worked on them? Are manuals, sample statements, or photographs available?

19. **What kinds of fraud or misuse caused trouble?** Lost cards, forged signatures, misuse by merchants, or something else? How were disputed transactions resolved, and who would remember the procedures best?
