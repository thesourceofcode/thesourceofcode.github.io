# Hotel credit cards: source notes

## Scope

Confined to cards issued by individual hotels and hotel chains. The association's Universal Travelcard and later Diners Club, American Express and Carte Blanche arrangements are excluded, following the user's direction.

## Sources used

- **Hotel New Yorker card, Heritage Auctions**, auction 7165, lot 52812: surviving signed card, catalogued to the 1930s, approximately 3½ × 2½ inches. [Catalogue](https://sports.ha.com/itm/football-collectibles/others/1930-s-joe-carr-signed-hotel-new-yorker-courtesy-credit-card-from-the-joe-carr-find/a/7165-52812.s). The catalogue date is approximate and does not establish the program's first year.
- **Western Hotels / Westin company history**: retrospective account dates paper cards to 1946 and describes charging rooms, food and beverages to a single account. [History](https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/westin-hotels-and-resorts-worldwide-history/). Did not repeat its broad claim of the first guest credit card, since earlier individual-hotel cards survive. No original 1946 announcement was located.
- **University of Houston Hospitality Industry Archives**: archivist describes Hilton's paper President's Card as postwar, and separately identifies earlier numbered keychain check-in objects. [Post and transcript](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/hiltoncollegeghl_mondayswiththearchives-hospitalityarchives-activity-7304875285814906880-nK63). No precise President's Card introduction year asserted.
- **Hilton Hotels Corporation, 1953 annual report**, p. 11, president's report dated 4 March 1954: direct contemporary evidence of both charge and cheque-cashing privileges. [University of Houston PDF](https://digitalcollections.lib.uh.edu/downloads/1v53jx80z?locale=en). Read its extracted text; the web screenshot endpoint failed. No monthly payment terms or revolving credit terms inferred.
- **Matty Simmons, The Credit Card Catastrophe (1995), pp. 71–73**, repository book: nearly one million Hilton hotel-card customers; 800,000 Sheraton customers with free credit cards; internal bookkeeping and collection. Figures attributed to Simmons; excluded his subsequent network negotiations.

## Repository sources consulted and limits

Read the existing `research/hotel-research.md` and relevant passages in Simmons, Mandell, Evans and Schmalensee, and Grossman's American Express history. The latter books principally concern later network competition, which is outside the final section's scope.

The existing hotel note cites Business Week, 21 July 1945, p. 53, for 70,000 New Yorker cardholders. The original page was not located in this pass, so the statistic is omitted rather than promoted from an unchecked note into a verified fact.

Explanations of customer recognition, convenience and repeat business are interpretation of the documented functions. No precise first-ever hotel-card claim is made. No new image is required for this text rewrite.
