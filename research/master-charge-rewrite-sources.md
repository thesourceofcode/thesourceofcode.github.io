# Master Charge rewrite: local-source audit

Research for the Master Charge section of `_posts/2026-05-15-payments-101-credit-card-chronology.md`, 17 October 2026. The requested starting point was `books_papers/THE ORIGINS OF MASTERCARD.pdf`; the narrative follows the article's Diners Club and BankAmericard sections. The maximum length is 75 percent of the current Diners Club section.

## Scope and method

Screened the existing extracted-text corpus of 48 PDFs under `books_papers` and 45 supplementary PDFs, chiefly from the local bank-card collections. Searches included `master charge`, `mastercard`, `interbank`, `western states bank`, `Hinke`, `Bontems`, `Benovitz`, `Tanenhaus`, and `Footman`. A hit was a relevance lead, not evidence that an entire book had been read. In particular, generic references to interbank banking or settlements were not treated as references to the card association.

The substantive reading concentrated on Ferreira's chapters 8–11, pp. 86–132, with the methodological discussion on pp. 25, 27–28; card-design appendix, pp. 217–228; original promotional material, pp. 229–237; the contemporary trade-journal reprint, pp. 244–250; and the associated source notes, principally pp. 272–284. Ferreira's printed and PDF page numbers coincide. The thesis combines contemporary documents, specimens, family archives and later interviews; these types of evidence have different weight.

Visually inspected rendered thesis pages 96, 107, 227, 229–237 and 244, as well as the existing California launch advertisement. This was necessary because the cardholder FAQ is image-only in the extracted text, and image orientation and card designs cannot be checked from search results. Text alone also concealed the launch advertisement's printed page label, B8.

## Cross-checks in other local books and reports

- **Sean H. Vanatta, _Plastic Capitalism_ (2024)**, local filename beginning `Plastic Capitalism_ Banks, Credit Cards, and the End of`. Read relevant passages at PDF pp. 134, 139–143 and 150, corresponding to printed pp. 91, 96–100 and 107. Particularly useful for the organizational choices, Hinke's dealings with Bank of America, and the relationship between regional groups and national interchange.
- **Lewis Mandell, _The Credit Card Industry: A History_ (1990)**, local filename beginning `The credit card industry _ a history -- Mandell, Lewis`. Read printed pp. 31–38 and 44–46 (PDF pp. 61–68, 74–76). Corroborates the weak common identification under Interbank and the 1969 acquisition of Master Charge rights. Its compressed narrative should not be used to date the circles' creation to 1969.
- **David L. Stearns, _Electronic Value Exchange_ (2011)**, local filename containing `Electronic Value Exchange_ Origins of the VISA Electronic Payment System`. Read printed pp. 27–28, 38, 55–59, 84–85, 98, 109 and 113. Particularly useful for the small “i,” the problem of recognizing locally named cards and cooperative processing. Its focus is Visa, so Ferreira's documentary chronology takes precedence for the earlier Louisville and California businesses.
- **David S. Evans and Richard Schmalensee, _Paying with Plastic_, second edition**, local filename beginning `David S. Evans, Richard Schmalensee - Paying with Plastic, 2nd Edition_`. Read printed pp. 59, 62–66 (PDF pp. 77, 80–84). Useful for regional cooperation, the 1968 Eurocard agreement, common branding, and the 1979 change to MasterCard.
- **Federal Reserve Board, _Bank Credit-Card and Check-Credit Plans_ (July 1968)**, `bank_credit_cards/bank_credit_card_report.pdf`, especially printed pp. 8–9. Useful contemporary background on interchange and bank participation. Banks that recruited merchants or acted as agents were not necessarily card issuers; association membership totals cannot automatically be described as issuing-bank totals.

## Discrepancies and editorial decisions

1. **There is no single launch date for everything.** Louisville's commercial system began in 1964; the Buffalo interchange initiative and California association date to 1966; California's common card launched in July 1967; the national association bought the marks in April 1969. The previous paragraph collapsed these into a founding followed by a 1969 merger. The replacement separates the events.
2. **1964 month uncertain.** Ferreira's main text says November; the contemporary 1969 trade article says December. Use the year alone.
3. **Name collision timing uncertain.** The trade article says three million cards were already distributed; Ferreira infers the discovery may have occurred before launch. Preserve the Eastern Airlines episode without giving it an exact month or saying it happened before a specific number of cards was printed.
4. **Name-search story is not a population-ranking fact.** Footman's claim that the search covered the forty biggest cities and Louisville ranked forty-first is memorable but unverified. Omit the ranking.
5. **Acquisition counts conflict.** Sources give different Louisville cardholder and merchant totals in 1967. The article uses the 1965 advertisement's explicit “over 100” and avoids disputed acquisition totals.
6. **Founding rosters vary.** Avoid a precise founding-bank count for Interbank and avoid treating California's four banks as the entire Buffalo founding group. Also distinguish the 1966 organizing initiative from later incorporation and the 1968 corporate reorganization.
7. **The circles predate national ownership.** California card specimens on p. 107 and the July 1967 advertisement show the overlapping circles before the 1969 transaction. Their absence from some locally named Interbank cards does not mean Interbank issued a single universal “i-only” card. Some early California specimens display the “i” on the reverse.
8. **The 1969 event concerned marks and licensing.** Hinke's letter identifies the object and consideration directly. Do not replace it with an unsupported corporate-merger account. The $25 contest prize, $300,000 assignment and Louisville's $45,000 entitlement describe different things.
9. **1979 versus 1980.** Mandell's brief account gives 1980; Evans and Schmalensee explicitly give 1979. The article follows the latter for the MasterCard renaming. It does not infer that every existing card was replaced simultaneously.
10. **Promotional savings are not audited findings.** The software article advertises $10,000 versus $100,000 and installation in as little as 27 days versus four person-years of development. These are attributed supplier examples, not independently verified typical savings, and are omitted from the narrative.
11. **No instant worldwide universality.** Agreements and memberships expanded potential acceptance, with regional and operational qualifications. Avoid turning them into a claim that every participating card immediately worked everywhere.
