Scope: all 33 PDFs and the newspaper clipping currently in `books_papers` were screened for this revision. Searchable text, contents and relevant passages were reviewed; image-only newspaper reports and the relevant *Origins of Value* pages were OCR-read. The previously followed Thornton handbill and Muir Google Books reference remain cited. Thornton establishes an early weekly-payment advertisement, not a proven global first. Muir’s linked p. 121 discusses instalment selling and style changes for Terry-style clocks.

Dating and interpretation choices:

- Nugent’s founding/instalment date for Cowperthwaite is attributed to him; other accounts describe evidence by 1812.
- Singer did not invent instalment buying. Bissell supplies the 1856/1857 chronology; the Harvard case supports the collection machinery without importing its broad priority claims. Black’s undated Indochina anecdote is explicitly attributed to his 1961 account.
- The legal discussion follows Hyman’s account of state-level cases, without repeating Thelen’s description of GMAC v. Weinrich as a Supreme Court case.
- A running ledger, a charge account due in full and a modern revolving line are distinguished; rural down payments plus harvest-due notes are not automatically called periodic instalments.
- Quote transcriptions were checked against source text/scans. Typographic apostrophes are normalized in the Clark quotation.
