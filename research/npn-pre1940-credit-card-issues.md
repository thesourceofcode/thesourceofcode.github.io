## How to use this list

- Dates are issue dates, not necessarily dates on which a scheme began. Monthly issues retain month-only dates.
- Labels describe the relevant content; they are not article titles unless an article heading is explicitly supplied. Article headings are normalized from OCR.
- Page numbers appear only where located in the text or established during source review. Otherwise use the linked full text and its suggested search words, then inspect the scan. Printed page numbers and scan-image positions differ.
- A courtesy or identification card may authorize credit, identify a commercial discount, or serve another purpose. Each entry describes the evidence rather than treating every card as a credit card.
- Coupon books can be prepaid, purchased on account, or exchanged between suppliers. Their inclusion does not by itself establish a credit-card scheme.
- Government entries include proposals and procurement negotiations as well as operating arrangements. Litigation entries describe allegations or testimony, not independent findings of fact.

## Coverage and limitations

[Collection](https://archive.org/details/pub_npn-national-petroleum-news). The Internet Archive catalogue query was `collection:pub_npn-national-petroleum-news AND year:[1900 TO 1939]`.

The catalogue returned **1,267 items**, dated 1909–1939. OCR was successfully retrieved for **1,263**. A search allowing flexible whitespace and hyphenation looked for credit, charge, courtesy, identification, travel and touring cards; credit/charge plans, systems and schemes; charge accounts; and gasoline/fuel coupon or scrip books. It also recognized several common OCR misspellings of “credit”. The 236 issues producing these keyword matches were screened for relevant material; the lists above retain substantive examples and useful brief references, rather than every occurrence of generic credit language.

This is a research finding aid, **not a claim that these are every relevant issue**. Poor OCR, illegible advertisements, words outside the search vocabulary, collection gaps and the four retrieval gaps can hide additional material. The earliest detailed card plan found in this scan is the Tulsa Oil Products report of 23 March 1921; this does not establish the first oil-company card or disprove an earlier launch. The 1915 material concerns coupons and charge accounts, not an identified credit card.

Examples excluded from the card list include employee security passes (March 1917), metaphorical use of credit cards (15 July 1921), film acknowledgments (24 March 1926), salesmen’s credentials (21 July 1926), parking passes (30 May 1928), car-wash privileges (5 and 12 March 1930), committee membership cards (1 November 1933), non-oil-company cards (14 August 1935; 29 September 1937), and office credit-record cards (9 March and 27 July 1938). The 12 April 1939 Greenbelt discussion considers introducing cards but does not report an operating scheme.
