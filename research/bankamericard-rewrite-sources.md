## Source differences and editorial choices

- Fresno mailing: Vanatta gives nearly 60,000; Stearns gives 65,000. Use approximately 60,000, disclose the difference in a footnote.
- Credit screening: unsolicited did not mean literally no screening. Vanatta describes established customers and explicitly quotes the bank acknowledging limited checking during expansion. Avoid claims of no credit checks or indiscriminate initial distribution to all residents.
- Scale: two million cards issued is not two million active customers. Vanatta explains the bank later revised the count to distinguish circulation from actual use.
- Profitability: Stearns reports May 1961 and explicitly records Mandell's concern that funding and advertising costs were omitted. Keep the reported date and qualification together.
- Plastic production: existing research in `evolution_res/bofa_computer_saga.md` describes a debossed system, but its launch chronology conflicts with the better established 1958 account. The article shows the early specimen and later embossed cards without using that account to date the switch precisely.
- IBANCO: Stearns gives June 1974 formation, while the *Ascent* paper describes September 1974 incorporation. The article uses the year alone.
- Visa: Stearns distinguishes May 1976 NBI board approval, August IBANCO approval, September announcement, and March 1977 formal renaming. Other summaries compress this into either 1976 or 1977. The article presents the stages explicitly. The Visa transition specimen is a later card expiring February 1983, not an authenticated 1977-issued specimen.
- Sumitomo: the supplied specimen says “VALID ONLY IN JAPAN.” Identify it as the domestic design introduced in 1968, not proof of international acceptance. Explain the separate overseas card.

## Enrichment from bank_credit_cards, 1 October 2026

The additions draw on the following supplied resources:

- Timothy Wolters, “Carry Your Credit in Your Pocket”: The Early History of the Credit Card at Bank of America and Chase Manhattan, *Enterprise & Society* 1 (June 2000), 315–354. Added the shelved 1953 metal-plate/paper-card proposal (325–327); the CSR team and October 1957 recommendations (328–331); March 1958 announcement and the exact three-page Fresno Bee launch advertisement (332); Leif's objections and March–October 1959 rollout (332–333); Wagele's collections, anti-fraud, processing and pricing changes and 1962–66 earnings (333–335); Chase's 1961 accounting comparison (346–347). A rendered copy of p. 332 was visually checked against the text extraction.
- *Commercial West*, 14 February 1959, p. 36, “Charge Plan a Hit.” Contemporary confirmation that statewide expansion had been publicly announced by February 1959.
- *Bank News*, December 1966, p. 21, “Bank-Issued Card in Operation.” May 1966 national licensing announcement; ten licensees from Boston to Honolulu; each issuer retained its lending risks and billing responsibilities.
- A. R. Zipf, “A Practical View of Universal Credit,” *Datamation*, March 1966, 121–122. Added the contemporary cost objection to replacing 55,000 imprinters with hypothetical $200 terminals. The $11 million calculation is not an actual capital expenditure. Zipf also distinguishes electronic authorization from repayment risk and discusses difficulties of descriptive billing.
- Federal Reserve System, *Bank Credit-Card and Check-Credit Plans*, July 1968, p. 12. Added industry context: over 14 million accounts, 35 percent active, as of September 1967. Avoid treating these as BankAmericard-specific counts. The separate June 1968 task-group appendix in `credit_card_description_1968.pdf` was also examined but its bank-specific table figures were not needed for this narrative.
- James L. McKenney with Duncan C. G. Copeland and Richard O. Mason, *Waves of Change* (1995), 66–67. Consulted the supplied original instead of relying on the existing extracted research notes. It describes a debossed system and later computer revisions, but its Fresno and metropolitan rollout dates conflict with contemporary evidence in Wolters; these dates are not adopted.
- John M. McGuire, *St. Louis Post-Dispatch*, 11 May 1973, p. 3C, “Credit Card Firms Have New Systems On Credit Clearance.” Visually read the supplied page; added the processing-center network and nights/weekends coverage. Archive filename `Page_67` is not the printed page number.
- *Burlington Free Press*, 14 November 1974, p. 6, “New System for BankAmericard.” Visually read the supplied article. Dates nationwide BASE II operation to 1 November and describes replacing mailed drafts. Retain the distinction between its promise of eventual paper elimination and immediate complete adoption.
- Bank of America advertisement, *Record Searchlight*, Redding, 22 February 1977, p. 7. Visually read and copied unchanged as `assets/images/credit-card-chronology/bankamericard_visa_mastercharge_ad_1977.jpg`. Contemporary illustration of BankAmericard “with Visa added,” alongside Master Charge, and evidence of public use of the Visa mark before the March organizational renaming.

The most material revision concerns automation: the article previously repeated Stearns's categorical “computerized from the beginning” account. Wolters describes Williams's recommendation to defer electronic equipment and the introduction of limited automation under Wagele. The revised narrative describes the stages without assigning an unverified exact start date for computerized card accounting, and the footnote identifies the disagreement explicitly.

The 1976–77 Visa chronology remains based on Stearns's detailed account. The new 1977 advertisement demonstrates how the rollout looked in public without implying that all banks changed names and replaced cards simultaneously.
